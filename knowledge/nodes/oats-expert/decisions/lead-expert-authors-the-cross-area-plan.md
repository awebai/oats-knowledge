# Direction for the lead expert

For cross-area work, the lead expert writes **one integrated plan** and
coordinates its execution by default. The lead is the generalist where one
exists, otherwise the expert whose area contains most of the effort. It reads
the affected domain nodes and asks other experts read-only questions before
writing; it does not delegate authorship of the whole to a coordination-only
seat or assemble independently chosen area plans afterwards.

The plan names each interface and its owner and records the agreement before
building. Each domain expert reviews its section before implementation, then
writes its own executable specification within that plan. The human reviews
the integrated direction once in the lead's conversation, rather than being
asked to reconcile separate area plans.

The same lead spawns and sequences the work, keeps the effort's state in a
file, integrates and closes. Relieve context pressure first with the state
file, then a clean respawn of the lead, and only then a second seat. A separate
coordinator is the exception for an epic whose experts and people outgrow the
lead's context: it tracks, integrates and closes, but does not take over
planning, research or gate authority. This is a qualitative threshold, not a
measured number of agents or people.

# Why this choice, and what it rejects

The source reports two reasons for keeping authorship and coordination
with the expert who holds the whole:

- Independently sound area plans can leave the interfaces between them
  unowned; resolving those interfaces belongs in the shared plan, not in
  a later assembly pass.
- Giving another seat the plan duplicates the lead's context and adds a
  relay before the extra coordination capacity is needed.

These are the rationale for the chosen default, not measured effects of
this trial. Domain expertise still matters: reading the nodes, asking for
constraints and obtaining section review are not replaced by the lead's
single authorship. Local specifications are elaboration, not a return to
independent planning followed by assembly. The narrower
[shared-design lesson](/nodes/oats-expert/lessons/inventory-then-one-shared-design-for-a-cross-client-redesign.md)
retains the two-client example rather than becoming a second home for this
authorship decision.
