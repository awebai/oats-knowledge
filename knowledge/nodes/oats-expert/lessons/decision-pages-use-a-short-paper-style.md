# The page is for deciding, not reconstructing the investigation

The source reports three corrections from Pepe during one coordination-design
effort on **2026-11-02**: replace comparison columns and tables with full
sections; shorten the subsequent paper-style draft and foreground practices,
what will exist and why; and replace an unfamiliar role name with a plain
word Pepe understood. Changing the layout alone did not cure the excessive
length.

For the next decision page addressed to this human, start with a short,
single-column, paper-style document:

1. Brief abstract and the problem.
2. The verified practices relevant to that problem, with evidence limits
   kept visible.
3. One section per proposed element: **Practice / What we will have / Why**.
4. Worked examples for the intended use cases.
5. A numbered list of decisions needed.
6. Sources last, linking the longer evidence separately.

Use familiar words for the kinds of agents or roles. Prefer numbered
sections over comparison grids, tables or a running Now/Proposed history.
The source's suggestion to cut a first draft roughly in half is an editing
prompt, not a universal length requirement or permission to omit a decision's
constraints. Keep investigation and verification history in the linked
evidence rather than making it the main narrative.

# Why, limits and prevention

The source's explanation is that top-to-bottom sections let the human follow
the proposed outcome and its rationale without reconstructing them from a
grid or the sequence of rewrites. An unfamiliar name consumes a clarification
round before the underlying choice can be discussed. These are lessons from
reported corrections, not measured reading-speed or decision-quality gains.

This is **one human and one effort**, not a universal ban on tables or a
format contract for every workspace, document or client. The draft's size,
version numbers and the rejected word are incidental; the reusable lesson
is to begin with the reader's decision and vocabulary. The broader question
of rendered-update effectiveness remains in the research node's
[oversight evidence gap](/nodes/market-research-expert/limitations/rendered-updates-oversight-evidence-gap.md),
not answered by this case.

A reusable decision-page template in the lead's authoring guidance is the
prevention route, for separate review. Structural checks can flag an absent
decision list or comparison grid, but cannot establish that the text is
concise, understandable or sufficient for this reader. No template or skill
change is delivered by this lesson.

Formatting does not decide who may approve an item. Before numbering the
choices, apply the
[existing-decision and supersession check](/nodes/oats-expert/lessons/ask-for-the-record-of-the-briefs-subject-before-designing.md).
The [structured-request prototype](/nodes/oats-expert/lessons/an-agents-request-to-a-human-is-data-drawn-once-in-the-efforts-thread.md)
concerns a client's request presentation, not this longer authored decision
page; neither record replaces the other.
