# Dated conclusion

The naming investigation of **2026-11-02** reports that agent products often
reuse ordinary terms rather than distinguishing a reusable definition with
one shared specialist noun. For a naming comparison, separate **what the
product calls an object** from **what persists, is copied, or is versioned**.
A familiar name does not establish a shared lifecycle contract.

This is a source-reported synthesis, not an independently audited product
inventory. The captured evidence does not establish that no product has a
distinct definition noun, that every vendor follows the same convention, or
that an unused search term is available as a product name.

# Reported distinctions worth checking

The captured note identifies these naming leads [1]:

- **Agent, custom agent, subagent and definition:** coding tools reportedly
  reuse agent labels for reusable configurations; the note identifies
  `AgentDefinition` in Microsoft AgentSchema and the Claude Agent SDK.
  It also reports a tendency to call active execution a session. Check each
  product's definition/execution distinction rather than infer it from the
  noun alone.
- **Template:** the source describes OpenClaw, LangSmith Fleet and Copilot
  Studio templates as creating copies rather than maintaining a link back
  to a shared definition. This is a reported lifecycle interpretation to
  verify, not a universal template contract or a measured reader response.
- **Soul:** the source describes OpenClaw's SOUL.md and Hermes's use of the
  term as voice/persona content rather than the whole reviewed agent
  definition. That product usage is a reason to test for a narrower reading,
  not proof that every reader understands the word that way.

The useful conclusion is that a naming recommendation needs a **semantic
comparison**, not just a list of apparently unused words. A proposed name
may already suggest a persona, a starter copy or a running object while the
intended concept is a shared reviewed definition. Explain that distinction
explicitly and test whether readers recover it. The separate
[role-name lesson](/nodes/market-research-expert/lessons/role-names-carry-product-meanings.md)
owns human-facing job labels and oversight, not definition lifecycle.

# Limits and application

The proposal reports a broad survey, but the permitted notes preserve
neither a complete product-by-product matrix nor primary-page URLs or
excerpts. Some items depended on search snippets, without item-level labels
in the captured notes. Treat the examples above as attributed research
leads: recheck decisive terminology and copy/version behavior against the
selected product and date before making a public comparison, following
[decisive-claim verification](/nodes/market-research-expert/lessons/verify-decisive-product-claims-before-synthesis.md).
No primary page was fetched for this harvest.

The proposal also reports lexical-frequency and vocabulary-list checks.
Those are familiarity proxies, not observed first-read comprehension; the
capture lacks the measurement records needed to reuse exact scores or
rank names. No controlled agent-definition naming test is established by
this investigation. No trademark or repository-filename clearance was
performed, and an unsuccessful product-name search proves neither novelty
nor legal availability.

This research does not choose an OATS name, certify its uniqueness or change
the accepted [durable-specialization rationale](/nodes/oats-maintainer/decisions/durable-specialization.md).
That decision keeps renaming open; market wording does not supersede it.

**Owner and recheck:** market-research-expert. Recheck the relevant product
usage before a naming decision or public comparison, and when terminology,
copy/lineage behavior or controlled reader evidence changes. Update or
supersede this dated conclusion and log the change rather than silently
turning the survey into a timeless market fact.
