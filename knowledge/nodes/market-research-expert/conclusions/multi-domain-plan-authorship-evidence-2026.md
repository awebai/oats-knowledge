# Dated conclusion

The **2026-11-02** investigation proposes a pattern for cross-area features:
one author holds the affected areas in context and writes an integrated plan,
including scope, interfaces and merge order; domain owners review it; local
specifications elaborate it. This is a **source-reported practice synthesis
and candidate design**, not a measured winning planning topology or a
universal description of current practice.

The source found neither a documented example of per-area planners whose
plans a coordinator assembles, nor a study comparing that arrangement with
one whole-plan author. The capture supplies no reproducible search inventory,
so these are gaps in this investigation, not proof that the alternative is
undocumented everywhere or ineffective. Plan authorship, implementation
parallelism and approval authority are separate variables.

# Contract ownership is more specific than a single-author label

The source attributes concrete cross-repository guidance to GitHub Spec Kit
repository discussion #4465 and issue #4609 [1]: an optional umbrella
specification plus per-repository specifications, a provider-owned interface
contract agreed by consumers, consumers pinning a contract version, and
merges ordered **contract, provider, consumer**. It reports no atomic
cross-repository specification in that account.

This is an attributed reading of a discussion and issue, not a checked
project-wide policy, product guarantee or evidence that all the specifications
must have one author. An accepted discussion answer does not by itself prove
a maintained enforcement contract. The useful research distinction is between
**who writes the integrated plan**, **who owns each interface contract** and
**who agrees to consume it**. A hierarchy of artifacts does not settle the
number of planners or demonstrate successful integration.

The source also describes an Abnormal AI engineering account, dated
2026-03-02, in which an agent drafts the whole design and domain teams supply
constraints and approve [2]. It supplies no baseline, measured model or
outcome comparison. That account is a practice lead, not evidence that owner
review or single authorship caused better results. Older RFC/KEP process
analogies likewise cannot establish an agent-planning outcome.

# Seam studies motivate contract checks, not an authorship verdict

Keep these source-reported readings separate:

- **CooperBench, arXiv 2601.13295**, identified as GPT-5 and Sonnet 4.5 [3]:
  the source reports lower success for two peers splitting work than for a
  solo agent, expectation mismatch among failure causes, and communication
  reducing merge conflicts without resolving the broader failures. The
  captured summary does **not** describe an explicit-contract intervention
  that fixes this study's failures.
- **The Specification Gap, arXiv 2603.24284**, dated March 2026 [4]: the
  source reports worse integration as specifications become less complete,
  recovery to the full-specification condition's ceiling when detail is
  restored, and no added benefit from conflict reports in that setting.
  It names Claude Sonnet and Haiku but gives **no exact model versions**.
  Restoring a measured ceiling is not evidence that every failure was fixed.

Both accounts concern work **within one repository**, not a multi-service
or cross-repository planning trial. The capture lacks full methods,
uncertainty and configuration detail, so no numerical effect sizes or
current-frontier result are adopted. Neither account compares one plan
author against per-area planners plus a coordinator, and neither establishes
that explicit contracts are the only possible remedy. Publication date does
not supply missing model identity or newer-generation transfer.

The separate [WideSWE account](/nodes/market-research-expert/limitations/cross-repo-architecture-ownership-evidence-gap.md)
owns cross-repository execution findings; it has no planner/coordinator arm.
The [topology lesson](/nodes/market-research-expert/lessons/topology-gains-depend-on-task-and-budget.md)
owns task, budget and intervention controls and other seam studies. Do not
pool these different experiments into one causal result about split ownership.

# Consequence for research, not a new approval policy

When asked who should plan cross-area work, offer the integrated-plan pattern
as an attributed candidate and state what remains unmeasured. Compare plan
authorship and access to affected-area context separately from contract
ownership, review, execution topology and budget. A proposed contract gate
needs executable integration checks in its owning implementation; a plan or
an ownership label is not behavioral verification.

Automatic teammate-plan approval already has a qualified canonical account
in [question routing and approval authority](/nodes/market-research-expert/lessons/question-routing-is-not-approval-authority.md).
Do not shorten conditional product behavior to a universal auto-approval
default, or infer human consent from agent approval. The source's proposed
human plan gate and prohibition on auto-approving local plans are not adopted
here. The [human-oversight conclusion](/nodes/market-research-expert/conclusions/human-oversight-evidence-2026.md)
owns review-timing hypotheses; none of this evidence selects an OATS planner,
changes the domain roster or supersedes an accepted review decision.
