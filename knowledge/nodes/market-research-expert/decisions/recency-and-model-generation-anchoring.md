# Decision and rationale

For research on agents, knowledge and coordination, prefer sources from
approximately the **last six months** and specifically search the **latest
three months**. Treat older sources as background to confirm or supersede,
not as automatically representative of current capabilities. These are rolling
windows relative to the research date, not a permanent May–November 2026 filter.

State the model generation behind every empirical finding. If evidence exists
only for an older generation, label transfer to current models **unconfirmed**.
Say whether newer evidence confirms, weakens or reverses the finding, or
whether no newer evidence was found in the investigation. When a source does
not identify the model, state that omission rather than supplying a guess.
Look specifically for evidence of how coordination needs change as models
improve; do not assume that every coordination or authority control disappears.

The reported rationale is that capabilities change fast enough for old or
generation-free findings to mislead current OATS decisions. Recency alone
does not establish quality: keep primary/secondary and experimental/observational
status visible, following the [decisive-claim verification lesson](/nodes/market-research-expert/lessons/verify-decisive-product-claims-before-synthesis.md).
The [scaffolding lesson](/nodes/market-research-expert/lessons/retest-scaffolding-after-model-changes.md)
owns the related controlled-retest conclusion.
