# Lesson

An agent role's local definition is not evidence that readers will infer its
intended function or authority. Before recommending a job label, compare
its current AI-product usage and everyday meaning with the responsibilities
it is meant to communicate. Keep three questions separate:

1. What do products and ordinary language use the word to mean?
2. What function and authority do people infer from it on a roster?
3. Does the label change task performance or how people check the output?

The **2026-11-02 source-reported naming investigation** motivates these
questions; it does not supply a controlled comparison of candidate OATS
names. The [persona-ablation lesson](/nodes/market-research-expert/lessons/persona-labels-need-isolated-ablation.md)
already owns attribution of agent-task gains to role wording versus
artifacts, instructions and feedback. That evidence does not settle
human interpretation or oversight.

# Product meanings are leads, not measured reader responses

The captured note reports planning-oriented uses of **architect** in Roo
Code, Aider and MetaGPT, contrasting them with a continuing design-record
steward. It also flags **routine** as a saved scheduled definition rather
than an agent kind, and **developer** as ambiguous between an agent and the
platform's human user [1]. These are reasons to check semantic collisions,
not universal definitions, a runtime authority audit or proof that all
readers make those associations.

The capture contains no itemized primary-source excerpts or links for these
examples, and the proposal warns that some research used search snippets.
Verify decisive usage against the selected product and date before citing
it publicly; the [verification lesson](/nodes/market-research-expert/lessons/verify-decisive-product-claims-before-synthesis.md)
owns that general research procedure. Lexical frequency and vocabulary-list
membership can help form familiarity hypotheses, but do not measure whether
someone understands a role's authority. Exact lexical rankings and categorical
claims that a word has no software meaning are not established here.

# A distinct oversight hypothesis

The source identifies BCG/HBR reporting dated **2026-05-06** as an experiment
comparing work labelled as coming from an **AI employee** with work labelled
as coming from an **AI tool**. It reports less human error detection and
more accountability attributed to the AI under the employee label [2].
This is a **secondhand qualitative research lead**: the proposer obtained
numbers through a BCG news page and Fortune, not the underlying report, and
this harvest inspected only the captured proposal and note.

The capture lacks a precise report locator, experimental protocol,
condition/denominator details and measured model identity. It therefore does
not support a reusable effect size, a universal job-title penalty, or a
causal claim about the particular role names above. An employee-versus-tool
framing comparison is not a multi-agent roster-comprehension test. It
motivates measuring human checking behavior separately from agent accuracy;
it does not prove that avoiding occupational titles improves oversight.
General human-gate and deference evidence remains in the
[human-oversight conclusion](/nodes/market-research-expert/conclusions/human-oversight-evidence-2026.md).

# Consequence for naming research

Prefer candidate labels whose plausible common reading matches the actual
function and authority, but treat that preference as a research judgment,
not a proven ranking of names. A one-line function gloss beside the label
is a candidate intervention to test, not an established remedy. A useful
comparison would vary the label and gloss separately and measure both
responsibility/authority comprehension and checking behavior.

The source reports finding no controlled study of names in multi-agent
rosters. That is an investigation-specific gap, not proof that no study
exists. Neither terminology searches, frequency proxies nor the secondhand
employee/tool finding settles it. No OATS role rename, authority change or
approval-policy change follows from this lesson.

**Owner and elimination route:** market-research-expert. Citation and
condition checks belong in separately reviewed research guidance and
structured evidence tooling; no soul skill is changed here. A lint can flag
missing evidence fields but cannot establish what readers infer. Controlled
reader/oversight evaluation is the route to replacing the current naming
hypotheses with evidence. Recheck on primary access to the reported study,
changed product usage or a controlled naming test; update or supersede this
lesson and log any changed conclusion.
